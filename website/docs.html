Overview

P-Sphere backs up folders between your own devices: Android phones and tablets, and Windows laptops and PCs. Your devices are the cloud. They talk to each other directly over your Wi-Fi, so there's no server, no account and no internet involved.

  • Several folders per device, each going to the devices you choose. For example, Camera from your phone to the tablet and the laptop, and WhatsApp Images to the laptop only.
  • Every device sends and receives. Each receiving device chooses where backups are saved.
  • Backup, not mirror. Deleting a file on one device never deletes its copies.
  • Only your linked devices can connect, and everything they send each other is encrypted.

P-Sphere is in early development. Keep another copy of photos you can't afford to lose until there's a stable release.

Quick start

Back up your phone to your laptop in a few minutes.

  1. Install P-Sphere on the phone and the laptop from the download page, and give each one a name, like “Phone” and “Laptop”.
  2. Connect both to the same Wi-Fi.
  3. On the laptop, open Devices → Link a device. A QR code appears.
  4. On the phone, open Devices → Link a device → Scan a code and point it at the laptop's screen.
  5. On the laptop, open Receiving and choose where backups go, for example D:\PSphere Backups.
  6. On the phone, open Sending → Add a folder, choose your Camera folder, tick Laptop and press Start backing up.

The first backup starts straight away. After that, P-Sphere backs up by itself whenever the two are on the same Wi-Fi.

Linking devices

Devices only back up with devices you've linked. You link each pair once: with a phone, a tablet and a laptop, that's up to three links (phone–tablet, phone–laptop, tablet–laptop).

With a QR code

Open Link a device on both devices. One shows a QR code, and the other taps Scan a code and scans it. A laptop has no camera, so it always shows the code. The code works once, for 10 minutes.

Without a camera

Under Link without a camera (on Windows: Can't scan? Link with a 6-digit code), devices on the same Wi-Fi with Link a device open are listed. Press Link. Both screens show the same 6-digit code; check that they match and press Codes match on both.

Unlinking

Open the device under Devices and choose Unlink. It stops all backups with that device, and it can no longer connect. Files already copied stay where they are. Unlink on the other device too.

Devices

The home screen. At the top, the status: Everything is backed up, Backing up with Tablet · 45 of 120, or Waiting for Laptop when a device that should get files is away. Back up now starts a backup straight away.

Below, a card for each linked device shows whether it's on this Wi-Fi, how many files are waiting to go to it, and how many of its files this device keeps. Open a card to see each folder going to that device, each of its folders kept here, Back up with … now and Unlink.

Sending: your folders

The folders on this device that P-Sphere backs up, and where each one goes. Add a folder asks for:

  • Folder. Picked with the system's folder picker. On Android, the root of Download and the Android/data folders can't be chosen; Android doesn't allow it.
  • Name. Your other devices keep the backup in a folder with this name.
  • Back up to. Tick the devices this folder goes to.
  • What to include. Photos and videos, or All files. Subfolders are always included.
  • Show on my other devices. For the photo library that's coming next. Turn it off for private folders; the folder is still backed up.

Open a folder to change any of this, or choose Stop backing up this folder. Copies already made stay where they are, and nothing on this device is deleted.

Receiving: your backups

  • Backup location. Where backups from your other devices are saved on this device. It must be chosen before other devices can back up here. Changing it doesn't move backups already saved; new backups go to the new folder.
  • Space. Free space where backups are saved. P-Sphere always leaves 1 GB free and stops receiving when it would go below that. The sending device then says, for example, “Laptop is running out of space. Some files weren't backed up.”
  • Pause receiving. Your other devices wait until you turn it off.
  • Kept here. Every folder this device keeps, grouped by the device it came from. On Windows, Open folder shows it in File Explorer.

A folder you back up from this device can't also be the backup location, or be inside it: backups would be sent straight back out. P-Sphere tells you when you pick one.

Where backups go

Inside the backup location, each device gets its own folder, and each of its folders keeps its name and subfolders:

<backup location>/<device>/<folder name>/<original subfolders and files>

D:\PSphere Backups\Phone\Camera\2026\IMG_0001.jpg
D:\PSphere Backups\Phone\WhatsApp Images\IMG-20261020-WA0003.jpg
Pictures/PSphere/Tablet/Documents/report.pdf

Files that arrive through another device still land in the folder of the device they came from originally: a phone photo that reached the laptop through the tablet goes to Phone\Camera.

When backups run

  • Automatically, whenever two linked devices are on the same Wi-Fi and one has something the other should have.
  • On Android, in the background, every few hours (6 by default), on Wi-Fi.
  • On Windows, while P-Sphere is open or in the tray.
  • By hand, with Back up now, for all devices or one.

If a device is off or away, its files wait and the status says so, for example “Waiting for Laptop · 312 files”.

Settings

  • Name of this device, as your other devices see it.
  • Only while charging (Android): background backups wait for the charger. Back up now always works.
  • Back up in the background every 1, 3, 6, 12 or 24 hours (Android).
  • Stay ready to receive (Android): keeps P-Sphere running, with a notification, so other devices can back up here any time. Best on a tablet that stays at home.
  • Linked devices and About (version and device ID).

Tray, start with Windows

The Windows app has the same sections as the Android app, in a side menu. It also:

  • Keeps running in the tray when you close the window, so your devices can keep backing up to it. The P icon at the bottom right opens a menu: Open PSphere, Back up now, Pause receiving and Quit PSphere. You can turn this off in Settings; closing the window then quits.
  • Starts with Windows if you turn it on in Settings. It opens in the tray.
  • Shows notifications when a backup finishes, for example “Backed up 120 files from Phone”, or when a device is running out of space.
  • Opens the running window if you start it again, instead of starting a second copy.

P-Sphere keeps its own data (device identity, links, settings) in %LOCALAPPDATA%\PSphere. Settings → About has an Open folder for it.

Windows Firewall

The first time P-Sphere runs, Windows asks whether to allow it on networks. Allow it on Private networks; that's what lets your phone find and reach the laptop. If you chose Cancel by mistake, open Windows Security → Firewall & network protection → Allow an app through firewall and tick P-Sphere for Private.

Your home Wi-Fi must be set as a private network in Windows (Settings → Network & internet → Wi-Fi → your network → Private network). On a public network, Windows blocks devices from finding each other.

The rules it follows

  1. Backup, not mirror. Deleting a file on its device never deletes a copy anywhere else.
  2. Only your devices. A device talks only to devices you've linked. Other P-Sphere devices on the same Wi-Fi are ignored.
  3. Each folder has its own destinations. Nothing is sent to a device the folder's settings don't name.
  4. The receiver decides where files go. A sender never chooses where a file lands.
  5. Any copy can be the source. Files are identified by their contents (SHA-256), so a file is never stored twice, and any device holding it can pass it on.
  6. Nothing is lost silently. Every file is checked after it arrives. A failure is shown with the device's name and the reason.

Relay and resume

Relay. If the laptop was off while the phone backed up to the tablet, the laptop catches up from the tablet later, as long as the phone's folder also goes to the laptop. The phone doesn't need to be there.

Resume. Files are written to a temporary file, checked against their fingerprint, and only then put in place. A large video that was interrupted continues from where it stopped instead of starting over.

Changed and deleted files

  • Deleted on its device: the backups stay.
  • Changed on its device (same name, new content): the new version replaces the backup, and the old version is moved to <backup location>/.psphere-old/ and deleted after 30 days.
  • Different files with the same name can't clash, because each device and each folder has its own folder in the backup location.

Security

Each device creates a key pair the first time it starts; its device ID comes from the public key. Linking swaps public keys: the QR code carries the key's fingerprint, and the 6-digit code is worked out from both keys, which is why both screens must show the same one. Every connection afterwards uses TLS, and each side checks that the other's key is the one it linked with. So everything sent is encrypted, and a device that isn't linked can't connect.

Backups are saved as ordinary files on the receiving device, not encrypted there. Anyone who can unlock that device can open them; use the device's own encryption (Android does this by default, BitLocker on Windows) if that matters to you.

Found a security problem? Please report it privately, as explained in the security policy.

Finding each other

Devices announce themselves on the local network with mDNS (service _psphere._tcp), with their device ID and protocol version, and look for each other the same way. Each device also remembers the last address of every linked device and tries it directly, because some routers block mDNS.

Troubleshooting

My devices don't find each other

  • Check they're on the same Wi-Fi network, not one on a guest network.
  • On Windows, allow P-Sphere through the firewall and make sure the Wi-Fi is a private network (see Windows Firewall).
  • Guest, office and public networks often stop devices from seeing each other (“client isolation”). Try your home router, or a phone's hotspot.
  • Turn off VPN apps; many send local traffic away from the Wi-Fi.
  • On a phone, pull down on the Devices screen to search again.

“Couldn't reach Tablet”

The device was found but didn't answer. Open P-Sphere on it and try again. Some phone makers stop background apps aggressively: exclude P-Sphere from battery optimisation, or turn on Stay ready to receive on a device that should always accept backups.

“Has an older version of PSphere”

Version 2.0 doesn't work with 1.x. Update P-Sphere on every device.

“Can't open this folder”

The folder was moved, renamed or deleted, or it's on a drive that isn't plugged in. Open it in Sending and choose it again.

“Laptop is running out of space”

The receiving device stopped because only 1 GB would be left. Free up space there, or choose a backup location on a bigger drive.

Windows says “Windows protected your PC”

The installer isn't code-signed yet, so Windows doesn't know the publisher. Choose More info, then Run anyway.

FAQ

Does P-Sphere work without internet?

Yes. It only needs a local Wi-Fi network; whether that network is connected to the internet doesn't matter.

Do I need an account or a server?

No. There's no P-Sphere account and no server, yours or ours.

Does it back up videos and other files?

Yes. Choose Photos and videos or All files for each folder. Large videos take a while the first time, and continue where they stopped if interrupted.

Will deleting a photo on my phone delete the backups?

No. P-Sphere is a backup, not a mirror.

Is there an iPhone or Mac version?

Not in this version. P-Sphere runs on Android and Windows.

How do I report a bug?

Open a bug report on GitHub. Say which devices were involved (make, model, Android or Windows version, and the version from Settings → About) and how they were connected.

Build from source

P-Sphere is open source under the GNU AGPL-3.0. You need JDK 17 or newer (the one in Android Studio works) and, for the Android app, the Android SDK.

git clone https://github.com/Madhuhas/P-Sphere.git
cd P-Sphere

# Shared core: run the tests
cd shared && ./gradlew test

# Android app: app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk
cd ../mobile && ./gradlew assembleDebug

# Windows app: run it, or package it (packaging needs a full JDK with jpackage)
cd ../desktop-app && ./gradlew run
./gradlew packageMsi

The contributing guide explains the project layout and how to test changes.

Privacy

P-Sphere has no analytics, crash reporting or advertising, and never contacts a server run by the P-Sphere authors. Your devices talk only to each other, on your own network. On Android, scanning a QR code uses Google Play services' code scanner, which runs on the phone; P-Sphere doesn't send it your files.

Changelog

Every change as it happens is in the update log on the home page.

2.0 (early development)

  • A new design: your own devices back each other up. Several folders per device, each going to the devices you choose; every device sends and receives; each device chooses where backups go.
  • Linking with a QR code or a 6-digit code. Only linked devices can connect, and everything is encrypted.
  • Relay through any device holding a copy, and resume for interrupted transfers.
  • The Windows app: the same sections, in the tray, can start with Windows, with an installer.

  • Not compatible with 1.x: update every device.

  • Phone-to-phone sync of one folder over Wi-Fi, with an optional home server.
