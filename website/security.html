Security

How PSphere keeps your backups private

Your files move only between your own devices, over your home Wi-Fi, and they are encrypted the whole way. We tested this on real devices, and tried to break it.

In short. Every backup is sent over TLS 1.3 with AES-256-GCM encryption. Only devices you have linked can connect, and each one checks the other by a pinned key that cannot be forged. In testing on a Windows laptop and an Android phone, no file content could be read from the network, and every attack we ran was turned away.

What protects your files

  • Encrypted in transit. Backups travel over TLS 1.3 with AES-256-GCM, so anyone watching your Wi-Fi sees only scrambled data.
  • Only your devices. Devices prove who they are with keys swapped when you link them. A device you have not linked cannot connect, even on the same network.
  • No middle-man. Each device pins the other's unique key, so nobody can pose as one of your devices to read or alter what you send.
  • You stay in control. Linking is a deliberate step (scan a QR code or compare a 6-digit code), and unlinking a device cuts it off straight away.
  • No server, no account. There is nowhere central to attack. Your files are copied straight to your other devices and stored there.

Can anyone read your files on the Wi-Fi?

No. We put a line of fake sensitive text inside a backup, sent it between two devices, and recorded every packet. Here is what the file contains, and what actually crosses the network:

In your file:     sensitive-bank-balance=9999999
On the network:   bc cd f0 4e 58 a4 9b 42 f5 19 e7 da a7 c4 53 2f ...

The content becomes meaningless scrambled bytes. We searched the whole recording for the secret text and it never appeared. Without the one-time key your devices agree on, there is nothing to read.

We tried to break in

We ran the kind of attacks someone on your Wi-Fi might attempt. Every one was stopped.

AttemptResult
Read your files out of the packetsBlocked
Force a weaker, older encryptionBlocked
Pretend to be one of your devicesBlocked
Sit in the middle and tamper with a transferBlocked
Connect without being linkedBlocked
Crash it with junk dataBlocked

Even a fake device that copied the right name was rejected, because PSphere checks each device's unique key, which an impostor cannot reproduce. A few smaller hardening points we found during the test were fixed straight after.

Tested on real devices

The test was run on a Windows laptop and an Android phone on the same home Wi-Fi, by capturing and inspecting PSphere's own traffic during live backups. The same encryption was confirmed on both.

Found a problem?

PSphere is open source, so anyone can review exactly how it works in the source code. If you believe you have found a security issue, please report it privately so it can be fixed before it is made public.
